### Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

**Why does aluminum stick to the permanent mold during ingot casting?**
Aluminum can adhere to the permanent mold when there is no stable release film or when it degrades due to high temperature, repeated cycles, or incorrect application. Adhesion can also increase due to mold oxidation, surface contamination, coating wear, or loss of lubricant properties between cycles. Proper process lubrication helps maintain ingot quality and process stability.

**How many filling and emptying cycles before reapplying release agent in permanent aluminum molds?**
There is no fixed number of cycles for reapplying a release agent in permanent aluminum molds, as frequency depends on the industrial lubricant, mold temperature, ingot design, and severity of the foundry process. Release agents formulated for dry film and high thermal stability can maintain their effect for several cycles, provided the lubricating film retains its integrity and does not degrade due to temperature, mechanical contact, cleaning, or process contamination.

**What is the difference between using a graphite-based release agent and a salt-based one in permanent molds?**
In aluminum foundry, graphite-based release agents usually offer greater release capacity and thermal resistance, favoring processes with intensive cycles and high temperatures. Salt-based release agents normally generate cleaner films and lower visible residue transfer to the ingot or mold. Lubricant selection depends on the required balance between release power, process cleanliness, and operating conditions.

**What problems does poor release agent application cause in aluminum ingot casting?**
Poor release agent application can generate a discontinuous film, unlubricated zones, or excessive lubricant buildup. This can cause aluminum adhered to the mold, surface defects on the ingot, variability in mold release, greater mold wear, and production stops for cleaning or process adjustment.

**How does the release agent affect mold cleanliness?**
Release agent formulation directly affects the cleanliness of the aluminum ingot casting process. For example, a water-based, dry-film release agent helps reduce residues, vapors, and contaminant buildup on the mold, facilitating operation and equipment maintenance.

### Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

**In continuous casting, why does the surface of the aluminum billet stain?**
Stains on aluminum billets produced by continuous casting are usually related to contamination of cooling water by oil drag or stable emulsion formation. When the industrial lubricant emulsifies easily, the water loses oil/water separation capacity and can affect the surface appearance of the aluminum, especially in subsequent processes such as aging, rolling, or surface finishing.

**What causes the *hot boiling* phenomenon in aluminum billet forming?**
*Hot boiling* in aluminum casting or continuous casting processes occurs mainly when cooling water is saturated with oil, reducing its heat dissipation capacity. Lubricants with high emulsification tendency or excessive dosages can favor this phenomenon, generating localized boiling, thermal instability, and possible surface or structural defects in the billet.

**How does the type of lubricant influence subsequent water treatment?**
The industrial lubricant used in aluminum casting processes directly impacts water treatment efficiency. Lubricants with low emulsification tendency allow faster oil and water separation by flotation, settling, or filtration, reducing operating and maintenance costs. Conversely, stable emulsions make treatment difficult, increase contaminant load, and can affect process sustainability.

**What problems does soapy residue formation in graphite molds cause?**
Soapy residue formation in graphite molds used in aluminum continuous casting can clog graphite pores, affecting uniform lubricant distribution and continuity of lubrication during the process. This condition can cause deficient lubrication, irregular mold wear, surface defects on the aluminum billet, and variations in foundry process stability. Soapy residues are usually generated when industrial lubricants with high emulsification tendency or chemical reactivity with the system water are used, forming deposits that accumulate in the porous structure of the graphite.

### Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

**Why does aluminum stick to the dummy or container during extrusion?**
Aluminum adheres to the dummy in extrusion processes when initial release is deficient due to inadequate lubrication. This usually occurs when the release agent is not suitable for high temperatures, is applied incorrectly, or does not generate a uniform and stable film. Lack of an effective lubricating film causes superficial aluminum welding, generating production stops, frequent manual cleaning needs, and premature wear of extrusion equipment.

**What causes bubbles to appear in extruded profiles?**
Bubbles in extruded aluminum profiles usually originate from trapped moisture, thermal decomposition of the lubricant, or irregular aluminum release during process startup. The use of lubricants with low thermal stability or that generate gases upon decomposition can introduce visible defects on the profile surface from the first stages of extrusion.

**Why do surface stains appear on extruded aluminum profiles?**
Surface stains on extruded aluminum profiles are generally related to lubricant residues, excessive application, or irregular film formation during the lubrication process. These residues can transfer during extrusion, affecting surface quality and complicating subsequent processes such as anodizing, painting, or finishing.

**How does the release agent influence extrusion equipment cleanliness?**
The type of industrial lubricant used in aluminum extrusion directly impacts the cleanliness of the dummy, container, and butt shear. Lubricants that generate carbonized, sticky, or difficult-to-remove residues increase downtime, solvent use, and maintenance frequency. Conversely, low-residue lubricants facilitate cleaning and improve operational continuity.

**What good practices help improve surface quality in aluminum extrusion?**
Surface quality in aluminum extrusion depends largely on control of the lubrication process and proper selection of industrial lubricants. Good practices include using ready-to-use release agents without incorrect dilutions, uniform and controlled application, avoiding products that generate difficult-to-clean residues, and continuously monitoring the condition of the dummy and container. It is also advisable to use lubricants with low environmental impact and safe for the operator, which contributes to cleaner profiles, less rework, and greater process stability.

### Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

**Why do aluminum profiles come out stained after saw cutting?**
Stains on aluminum profiles after cutting usually originate from the use of lubricants that leave persistent residues or from excessive fluid application during the process. This problem is common when oils not designed for profile cutting are used. Semi-evaporative lubricants allow reducing surface residue and preventing effects in subsequent processes such as anodizing, aging, or assembly.

**Why is smoke generated during billet cutting with a saw?**
Smoke during aluminum billet cutting is generated when the industrial lubricant does not have adequate thermal stability for the process temperature, especially in hot cutting operations. Under these conditions, the fluid thermally degrades, producing vapors, carbonaceous residues, and contamination in the cutting area. The use of direct cutting oils formulated for high temperature allows reducing friction, controlling blade temperature, and minimizing smoke generation.

**What causes premature wear of carbide discs in cutting saws?**
Premature wear of carbide discs in aluminum cutting is associated with excess friction and poor heat dissipation at the contact point. This is usually a consequence of incorrect lubrication or inadequate fluid application. A correctly selected industrial lubricant forms a stable lubricating film, reduces metal-to-metal contact, and extends cutting disc service life.

**How does the lubricant affect cut finish and burr formation?**
The industrial lubricant directly influences aluminum cut finish by controlling friction, temperature, and saw blade stability. Inadequate lubrication can generate vibrations, material drag, and excessive burr formation. Conversely, a correctly selected fluid improves cut stability and allows obtaining cleaner, more uniform, and repeatable surfaces.

**What problems does poor cutting fluid application cause in saws?**
Poor cutting fluid application in aluminum processes can cause excess lubricant, contamination of the work area, increased smoke generation, and product waste. It can also affect cut stability and process efficiency. Application must be dosed and directed specifically to the contact point between the saw disc and the material, avoiding over-application.

**How to choose the correct fluid for hot or cold aluminum cutting?**
Cutting fluid selection for aluminum depends on process temperature, material type, and subsequent cleanliness requirements. In hot cutting, direct cutting oils with high thermal stability are recommended to control friction and prevent degradation. In cold cutting, soluble fluids allow improving heat dissipation, providing anti-corrosive protection, and maintaining process cleanliness. Proper industrial lubricant selection improves cut quality, tool service life, and operational efficiency.

### Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

**Why can chains in painting ovens stain aluminum profiles?**
Chains in painting or aging ovens can stain aluminum profiles when the industrial lubricant loses stability at high temperatures, decreases in viscosity, or presents runoff during operation. Under these conditions, the lubricant can drip or transfer from the chain to the profiles as they advance through the oven, directly affecting the surface quality of the aluminum before or after the painting process.

**How does chain lubricant degradation affect the aluminum profile painting process?**
Lubricant degradation in oven chains can cause direct contamination of aluminum profiles, affecting paint adhesion and uniformity. It can also generate residue accumulation on the chain, increasing friction, component wear, and the risk of unscheduled stops. Therefore, it is advisable to use a specialized lubricant that maintains stable viscosity under severe thermal conditions, presents low runoff tendency, and resists evaporation during continuous operation.

**What problems occur when bearing grease carbonizes in aging furnaces?**
Grease carbonization in aging furnace bearings generates solid deposits that directly affect lubrication and component performance. These deposits increase friction, raise operating temperature, and reduce bearing service life. As a consequence, premature failures, vibrations, excessive noise, and unscheduled oven stops may occur. Selecting a grease with high thermal stability and low carbonization tendency is key to maintaining process reliability.

**How does proper chain and bearing lubrication influence the continuity of the aluminum extrusion process?**
Proper chain and bearing lubrication in aluminum extrusion processes is key to guaranteeing operational continuity and process stability. Correct selection and application of industrial lubricants reduces friction, wear, and the risk of mechanical failures in critical peripheral components operating in ovens and auxiliary systems, decreasing the frequency of corrective maintenance and avoiding unplanned interruptions.

**What good practices should be considered when selecting lubricants for high-temperature applications?**
Industrial lubricant selection for peripheral applications at high temperatures should be based on actual operating conditions and specific process requirements. It is essential to consider the temperature range, lubricant thermal stability, its oxidation resistance, and its tendency to generate residues or carbonization. It is also recommended to use products designed for each specific application (chains or bearings) and apply controlled lubrication methods that avoid excess, process contamination, or efficiency losses.
